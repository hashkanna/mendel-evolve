## The 2-minute film: `media/mendelevolve_film_2min.mp4` (the submission video)

**Title**

MendelEvolve: which idea deserves the credit? (2-minute film)

**Description**

AI systems that evolve programs find good algorithms, but nobody can say which idea produced the result. MendelEvolve turns every idea a language model proposes into a named switch in the solver, and knockouts, switching one idea off on fresh seeds, measure what each is worth.

In one weekend: new lower bounds at 17 sizes of problem 60 from Tao and co-authors, each checked by three independent exact checkers; 58 points with no isosceles triangle in the 32 x 32 grid, where two papers state that 56 is optimal; and the best known circle packing from OpenEvolve's own starting program. Turned on our own records, the knockouts showed that the best single idea was one the engine had thrown away.

## The full film: `media/mendelevolve_film.mp4` (3:12)

**Title**

MendelEvolve: evolve ideas, not programs (the film, 3 minutes)

**Description**

Evolutionary coding agents such as AlphaEvolve rewrite whole programs and keep the high scorers. Afterwards, nobody can say which idea produced the result: Darwin without Mendel, selection but no genes. MendelEvolve makes every idea a gene, a named switch in the solver. A language model only invents; classical search tunes; knockouts on fresh seeds measure what each idea is worth.

In one weekend it found new lower bounds at 17 sizes of problem 60 from Tao and co-authors, 58 points with no isosceles triangle where two papers state that 56 is optimal, and the best known circle packing from OpenEvolve's starting program. Then we turned the knockouts on our own records: the engine's quick screens were too weak to see a fifth of a point, and at record scale (600 paired runs per idea) the best single idea was one the engine had thrown away, worth between +0.19 and +0.31.

Built at the London AI x Science Hackathon, 3 to 4 October 2026 (Track 1: AI Automated Discovery of Algorithms).
