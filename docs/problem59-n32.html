58 points in the 32 x 32 grid, no isosceles triangle

Tao et al., problem 59. The value stated in the literature for n = 32 is 56.

Choose as many points of the n x n grid as possible so that no three of them form an isosceles triangle, flat ones included: for distinct chosen points a, b, c, the distance from a to b never equals the distance from b to c. Two different sets of 58 points for n = 32:

Computed in your browser just now, in exact integers, from the coordinates on this page: every triple of points is tested for two equal squared distances.

  • Each set passes all 30,856 triples under four independent checks, among them DeepMind's own verify_construction from the problem's notebook, run unchanged.
  • The same definition reproduces every published optimal value from n = 4 to 10 by exhaustive search (6, 7, 9, 10, 13, 16, 18), and the same solver equals the published 28 and 48 at n = 16 and 27.
  • At n = 32 the search ends at 56 in 185 of 200 runs and at 58 in the other 15.
  • Where it came from: the seed solver with two of its own switches on, and compute. No evolved idea.

Raised with DeepMind as issue 10, as a discrepancy to be explained. Details and limits are in RESULTS.md; certificates are in the repository.
